What decides the cost of an express shipment

Express carriers bill on the greater of actual weight and volumetric (dimensional) weight. Volumetric weight is the parcel volume divided by a fixed divisor, so a light but bulky box can cost more than a dense heavy one. Divisors differ by carrier and service, and many express services use 5,000 cm³ per kg while the air-cargo convention is 6,000 cm³ per kg, so confirm the divisor in the carrier's own rate terms before comparing quotes.

The other large variable is who pays import duty and taxes. Under delivered-duty-paid terms the sender settles them in advance; under delivered-at-place terms the recipient is billed on arrival, often with a carrier handling fee. These terms are defined in the ICC Incoterms rules, and the declared customs value and product description on the commercial invoice drive the amount assessed.

  • Measure the packed carton, not the product.
  • State the HS code and the real transaction value; under-declaring risks delay, penalties or seizure.
  • For repeat shipments, compare the carrier's account rate to its published rate, since the gap is usually larger than the gap between carriers.

References: ICC, Incoterms® 2020 (International Chamber of Commerce); IATA, Cargo Services Conference Resolutions Manual (volumetric weight conventions); carrier rate and service terms published by DHL, FedEx, UPS and USPS.

Choosing an International Courier: DHL, FedEx, UPS and EMS Compared, and How to Ship Samples

For samples and small consignments, international express courier is faster and simpler than ocean freight. Private couriers such as DHL, FedEx and UPS and postal EMS differ in speed, pricing, customs support and tracking. This guide covers selection criteria, how charges are set and what to check before shipping.

Selection criteria

CriterionWhat to check
SpeedTransit days (often 1–5 business days) and regional variation

CustomsScope of import clearance service, duty payment (DDP or DAP)
TrackingReal-time tracking and notifications

RestrictionsBatteries, liquids, food and other restricted goods

How charges are set

Express rates use the greater of actual weight and volumetric weight. Volumetric weight is commonly length × width × height in cm divided by 5,000, though carriers differ. Fuel and remote-area surcharges and clearance fees are added. Check volume with the CBM guide.

Before you ship

  1. Check whether the goods are prohibited or restricted, such as batteries, liquids, food or medicines.
  2. State product name, quantity, unit value, HS code and origin accurately on the invoice.
  3. Declare a commercial value even for samples.
  4. Agree who pays duty and tax, recipient (DAP) or sender (DDP).
  5. Keep packaging from inflating volumetric weight.

Common mistakes

  • Declaring a sample at zero value and delaying clearance.
  • Using a vague description that triggers inspection.
  • Not calculating volumetric weight and getting a higher charge.
  • Not agreeing duty terms, so the recipient refuses delivery.
