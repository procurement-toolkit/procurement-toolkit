Frequently Asked Questions

What if the cost comparison and strategic scoring disagree?

This happens often in practice. If cost favors Buy but strategic importance is very high, teams frequently choose Make anyway, prioritizing long-term competitiveness over short-term cost. A disagreement between the two is a signal to discuss carefully, not a bug.

How is the breakeven volume calculated?

It's the volume at which both options' total cost (fixed + variable × volume) becomes equal. Above that volume, the option with higher fixed cost (usually Make) becomes cheaper; below it, the other option wins.

How should I set the weights?

There's no fixed answer. The common practice is to make the five weights sum to 100% and reflect your organization's priorities — for example, weighting IP protection higher in an industry where that matters most.

Make or Buy Decisions: Cost Comparison and Evaluation Matrix

A make-or-buy decision asks whether to produce a part or service in-house or purchase it. Choosing only on unit price overlooks investment, quality control, supply risk and core capability. This guide covers the cost comparison, a qualitative matrix and decision rules.

Cost comparison basics

The cost of making includes variable cost (materials and direct labour) and fixed cost (equipment and staff). The cost of buying is the purchase price plus logistics, inspection, management and transaction costs. Compare total cost at the annual volume you need. Making tends to win at high volume when fixed costs are large; buying tends to win at low volume.

If in-house production has fixed cost of 60 million a year and variable cost of 4,000 per unit while a supplier charges 7,000, break-even volume is 60,000,000 ÷ (7,000 − 4,000) = 20,000 units. The figures are illustrative.

Decision steps

  1. Define specification and annual volume.
  2. Calculate total cost for both options on the same basis.
  3. Weight and score the qualitative criteria.
  4. Test risks, such as supply interruption and quality failure, as scenarios.
  5. Combine the results, decide and set a review date.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving overhead and management effort out of in-house cost.
  • Missing hidden outsourcing costs.
  • Outsourcing core technology and losing the advantage.
  • Not checking whether the conclusion flips at a different volume.
