Term-by-Term Breakdown

Favors Buyer

T/T (Telegraphic Transfer)

  • Simple bank-to-bank wire transfer
  • Can be combined flexibly — advance, on delivery, or installments
  • Simple process, low fees
  • Can be risky with a new counterparty with no trust history
Balanced — Bank Guarantees Payment

L/C (Letter of Credit)

  • The buyer's bank guarantees payment
  • Bank commits to pay once documents (B/L, invoice, etc.) match
  • Common safeguard for large transactions with new counterparties
  • Issuance fees apply, and strict documentary requirements add work
Favors Seller

D/P (Documents against Payment)

  • Buyer must pay before receiving shipping documents
  • Bank handles the documents but does not guarantee payment
  • If the buyer doesn't pay, they cannot claim the goods
Risk to Seller

D/A (Documents against Acceptance)

  • Buyer only needs to accept a bill of exchange (a promise to pay later) to get documents
  • Buyer receives the goods before payment is due
  • Recommended only with a buyer whose creditworthiness is verified

Checklist Before Choosing Payment Terms

  • Do you have a trading history with this counterparty, or is it a new relationship?
  • Is the transaction large enough to warrant a bank payment guarantee (L/C)?
  • What is the foreign exchange or political risk in the counterparty's country?
  • If using L/C, does the buyer have sufficient credit line headroom to open it?
  • Do you know the process for handling a documentary discrepancy?
  • Does the cash recovery timing under these terms fit your cash flow plan?
This guide is a general reference to help you understand the concepts. For actual contract terms or interpreting specific L/C clauses, consult your bank's trade finance team or a qualified professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do T/T and L/C compare in terms of risk?

T/T (wire transfer) is simple and fast but carries no bank guarantee, making it riskier for the paying or receiving party depending on timing. L/C (Letter of Credit) has a bank guarantee behind it, which is safer but comes with more paperwork and bank fees.

What payment terms are safest with a brand-new supplier?

A common approach is a partial advance payment (e.g., 30% T/T advance) with the balance due before shipment or secured via L/C. As trust builds over repeated transactions, terms can gradually be relaxed.

International Payment Terms: T/T, Letter of Credit, D/P and D/A Compared, With Risk Controls

Payment terms decide who pays first and by what instrument. The same goods carry very different cash-flow and counterparty risk under advance payment than under a letter of credit. This guide compares the main methods, explains what to check in a letter of credit and gives a way to choose.

What to know about letters of credit

  • The international rules are ICC’s Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600).
  • Banks examine documents, not goods. Small discrepancies can lead to refusal.
  • A sight credit pays on presentation; a usance credit pays after a stated period.
  • Opening, confirmation and discrepancy fees are charged separately.

How terms affect cost

Advance payment ties up cash and carries financing cost, while open-account terms may be priced in by the seller. Letters of credit add bank fees. Compare total cost in the payment terms cost calculator and review currency risk in the FX exposure calculator.

How to choose

  • For a new supplier, consider a letter of credit or split payment.
  • With a proven supplier, you can negotiate open-account terms.
  • For large amounts, consider a letter of credit or trade credit insurance.
  • Settle currency and exchange-rate risk at the same time.
  • Write document requirements and payment timing into the contract.

Common mistakes

  • Prepaying the full amount to an unverified supplier.
  • Not checking that the bank account name matches the supplier.
  • Shipping when documents do not match the credit terms.
  • Leaving bank charges out of the cost.
  • Choosing currency and Incoterms separately so that quotes cannot be compared.
