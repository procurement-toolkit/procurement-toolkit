How the hedging cost is calculated

Forward points and annualized cost

The difference between the forward rate (F) and the spot rate (S) is the forward points. The period premium is (F − S) ÷ S, and the annualized figure multiplies it by the day-count basis ÷ tenor. For an importer paying in foreign currency, a forward above spot (premium) is a cost and a forward below spot (discount) is a gain. For an exporter receiving foreign currency, the direction is reversed.

Theoretical forward from interest rates

Under covered interest parity the theoretical forward is S × (1 + domestic rate × days/basis) ÷ (1 + foreign rate × days/basis). When the domestic rate is lower than the foreign rate, the forward sits below spot. Actual market forwards can differ because of supply and demand, so treat this value as a reference.

Choosing a hedge ratio

Locking 100% of an exposure also gives up the benefit if the rate moves in your favour. A common practice is to hedge committed orders at a high ratio and forecast volumes at a lower one. Agree the policy with your finance team.

Frequently asked questions

What do forward premium and discount mean?

A forward rate above the spot rate is a premium, and below it is a discount. The main driver is the interest rate difference between the two currencies.

Why annualize the hedge cost?

To compare hedges with different tenors on the same basis. The period cost is multiplied by the day-count basis ÷ tenor to express it per year.

How is the bank spread applied?

For imports (paying foreign currency) the spread is added to the forward rate; for exports (receiving foreign currency) it is subtracted. It is applied against the dealer, to give the actual locked rate.

Should I use a 360 or 365-day basis?

Conventions differ by currency. USD, EUR and JPY normally use 360 days and GBP 365. Follow the basis in your bank's quote.
