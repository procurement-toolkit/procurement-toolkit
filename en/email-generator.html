Frequently Asked Questions

How do I open a business email when I don't know the recipient's name?

'Dear Sir/Madam,' is a safe, standard choice when you don't have a name. If you know the name but not the title, 'Dear Mr./Ms. [Last Name],' is the common convention.

How do I follow up on a delivery delay without damaging the relationship?

Frame it as a status request rather than an accusation. A phrasing like 'Could you please confirm the current status and provide an updated shipping schedule?' is direct but stays professional and collaborative.

What should a quality claim email always include?

Be specific about the quantity or percentage affected, when the issue was discovered, and the resolution you're requesting (replacement, refund, or credit note). Mentioning that supporting photos or documents are available usually speeds up the supplier's response.

How to Write Procurement Emails in English: Structure and Phrases for RFQs, Delivery Chasers and Claims

Emails to overseas suppliers should be short and precise so that replies come quickly and misunderstandings are rare. Requests for quotation, order confirmations, delivery chasers, sample requests and claims each need different information and tone. This guide sets out the basic structure, what to include per situation and common phrases.

Basic structure

  1. Subject: state the purpose and a reference, for example "RFQ – Stainless Steel Pot, 5,000 pcs – Ref. PO-1234".
  2. Greeting and introduction: if it is the first contact, give your company and role in one line.
  3. Purpose: say what you need in the first paragraph.
  4. Details: list specifications, quantity, delivery and conditions.
  5. Request and deadline: say when you need a reply.
  6. Close: thank the reader and give contact details.

Useful phrases

  • Request: "Could you please send us your best quotation for the following items?"
  • Deadline: "We would appreciate your reply by [date]."
  • Status: "Could you confirm the current status of PO-1234 and the expected ship date?"
  • Claim: "We would like to report an issue with the goods received under PO-1234."
  • Close: "Please let us know if you need any further information."

Points to watch

  • Keep one request per email so that nothing is missed.
  • Use one format for dates and numbers, such as 15 Dec 2026.
  • Write facts, evidence and the solution you want rather than emotion.
  • Confirm agreements by email to keep a record.
  • Give contractual notices in the form the contract requires.
