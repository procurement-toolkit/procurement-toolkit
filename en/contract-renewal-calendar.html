    Frequently Asked Questions

    How many days before expiry should the notice period be?

    It varies by contract, but 30, 60, or 90 days is common in practice. Always check the actual renewal/termination clause in the specific contract for the exact period.

    Where is my contract data stored?

    It's stored only in this browser (local storage) and never sent to a server. It won't appear on other devices or browsers, so for anything important, export it as an .ics file and keep a separate backup.

    Do auto-renewing contracts need notice too?

    Yes — arguably even more so. Contracts with auto-renewal clauses often extend automatically on undesirable terms if you don't state your intent to terminate or renegotiate within the notice period.

    Managing Contract Renewal and Termination Notice Dates: Auto-Renewal Clauses and Reminders

    Supply contracts often renew automatically unless notice is given by a deadline, and one missed date can lock in terms you did not want. Managing the notice deadline, not the renewal date, gives you time to negotiate and review alternatives. This guide covers the clauses to check, how to calculate dates and how to keep control.

    Clauses to check

    ClauseWhat to check
    TermStart and end dates
    Auto-renewalWhether the contract extends without notice, and for how long
    Notice of termination or non-renewalHow many days in advance and in what form
    Price adjustmentIncrease terms and caps at renewal
    Minimum purchasePenalty for shortfall
    Early terminationGrounds and penalties

    Calculating the date

    Notice deadline = contract end date − notice period. If the contract ends on 31 December and notice must be given 90 days in advance, the deadline is 2 October. Without notice by then it renews. Allow time for quotes and alternatives by starting preparation 60–90 days before the deadline.

    Process

    1. Record end date, notice period and auto-renewal status of every supply contract in one place.
    2. Calculate the notice deadline and set calendar reminders.
    3. Review performance and market prices 90 days before the deadline (see the supplier scorecard).
    4. Secure alternative quotes to strengthen your position (BATNA guide).
    5. Give written notice after deciding and keep proof of delivery.

    Common mistakes

    • Recording the end date but not the notice deadline.
    • Giving notice by email when the contract requires registered post.
    • Losing the renewal date when the owner changes.
    • Missing a price increase that applies on auto-renewal.
