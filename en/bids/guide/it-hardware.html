IT hardware, networks & telecom: tender guide

Purchases of laptops, servers, network gear, printers, security appliances and telecom equipment.

At a glance

Warranty length, local installation and support, and compatibility are common conditions, so prepare your after-sales support plan along with the offer.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Purchases of laptops, desktops, servers, network gear, printers, security appliances and telecom equipment, plus internet and telephone services.
  • Examples include bulk IT supply for schools and hospitals and long-term internet service contracts.
  • Annual framework agreements are common.

Who buys

Government bodies, schools and universities, hospitals and offices of international organizations.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Compliance with specifications and compatibility.
  • Warranty length and local installation and support.
  • Delivery time and price.

Common mistakes

  • Not checking compatibility and power specifications such as voltage and plug type.
  • Leaving the warranty service arrangement out of the offer.
  • Missing export or customs controls on certain items such as encryption equipment.

Document checklist

  • Catalogue and specification compliance table
  • Manufacturer warranty and proof of supply
  • Description of local support
  • Delivery schedule

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

Must bids come directly from manufacturers?

Some notices accept authorized resellers. Check the eligibility clause.

Are telecom services included?

Yes, internet and telephony (VoIP) service contracts appear here.

What is a framework agreement?

A contract that fixes unit prices without fixing total quantity, with orders placed as needed.
