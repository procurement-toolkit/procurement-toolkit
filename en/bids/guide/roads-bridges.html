Roads, bridges, rail & ports: tender guide

Civil works and supervision tenders for roads, bridges, railways, airports and ports. Large notices often come from World Bank and regional development bank projects.

At a glance

Big works may require a prequalification step first, so check whether a separate prequalification notice exists before the main tender.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Civil works and supervision tenders for road paving and widening, bridges, tunnels, railways, airports and ports.
  • Large notices often come from projects supported by the World Bank and regional development banks.
  • Purchases of road-maintenance equipment and materials (asphalt, aggregate, bridge components) are included.

Who buys

National transport and public works ministries, road and rail authorities, port and airport authorities and development-bank project units.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Civil works references of similar scale and equipment holdings.
  • Financial standing and ability to provide guarantees.
  • Work programme and safety management system.

Common mistakes

  • Missing a prequalification notice for large works.
  • Bidding alone when a joint-venture requirement applies.
  • Not reviewing price-adjustment and payment terms.

Document checklist

  • Civil works licence and project references
  • Recent financial statements
  • Equipment list
  • Programme and safety management plan

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find development-bank project notices?

This site collects World Bank notices daily, and each bank also has its own procurement portal.

Is a joint venture needed?

Large works sometimes impose it. Check the eligibility clause.

What is prequalification?

A step before the main tender that assesses bidders’ qualifications first.
