Office supplies & furniture: tender guide

Supply tenders for office furniture, stationery and consumables, often as annual framework agreements by government bodies and international organizations.

At a glance

Framework agreements list many items without fixed quantities, so price lists and delivery frequency matter most.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Supply tenders for office furniture, stationery, consumables and fittings.
  • Government bodies and international organizations often issue them as annual framework agreements.
  • Bulk furniture supply notices also appear when new offices are built or relocated.

Who buys

Government bodies, municipalities, schools and offices of international organizations.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Specifications (material, dimensions, durability) and safety and environmental certification.
  • Unit prices, delivery frequency and delivery coverage.
  • Sample approval procedure.

Common mistakes

  • Price-list omissions and calculation errors because of the large number of items.
  • Not checking pack units and minimum order quantities.
  • Leaving assembly and installation costs out.

Document checklist

  • Catalogue and specifications
  • Unit price list
  • Sample submission plan
  • Delivery and installation plan

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

What is a framework agreement?

A contract that fixes unit prices without fixing total quantities, with orders placed as needed.

Is sample approval needed?

Some notices require samples to be submitted and approved.

Can small suppliers bid?

Yes, on lots with fewer items.
