Water & sanitation: tender guide

Works and supply tenders for water treatment, water supply networks, sewage treatment, irrigation and borehole drilling.

At a glance

Supply notices spell out specifications and certifications for pipes, pumps and valves, so reading the technical specification first tells you quickly whether you can bid.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Works and supply tenders for water treatment plants, water supply networks, sewage treatment, irrigation and borehole drilling.
  • Supply of pumps, valves, pipes, meters and water-quality instruments also belongs here.
  • Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) projects for camps and communities are included.

Who buys

Water utilities, municipalities, water-resources ministries, international organizations and development-bank project units.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Compliance of materials with specifications and certifications (material, pressure class).
  • Installation references and testing and commissioning ability.
  • Maintenance and spare-parts support.

Common mistakes

  • Not checking the standards required for pipes and pumps.
  • Leaving installation and commissioning costs out.
  • Insufficient proof of meeting water-quality or environmental requirements.

Document checklist

  • Material specifications and certificates
  • Installation references and licences
  • Test reports
  • Maintenance and spare-parts plan

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

What does WASH stand for?

Water, Sanitation and Hygiene.

Can I take part by supplying materials only?

Some notices are supply-only. Others bundle works, so check the scope.

Which certifications are required?

They differ by notice. Check the standards in the technical specification.
