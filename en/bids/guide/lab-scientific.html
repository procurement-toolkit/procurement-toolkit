Laboratory, scientific & measuring equipment: tender guide

Purchases of laboratory equipment, analytical and measuring instruments, microscopes and calibration services by universities, research institutes and health agencies.

At a glance

Installation, training, warranty and consumables supply are often evaluated as one package.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Purchases of laboratory equipment, analytical and measuring instruments, microscopes, reagents, calibration, and testing and inspection services.
  • Notices that outsource quality inspection and testing to external providers are also included.
  • They commonly come from research bodies and environmental or food-safety agencies buying equipment.

Who buys

Universities, research institutes, health and food-safety agencies and environmental agencies.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

UNSPSC segments

What evaluators look at

  • Compliance with the technical specification and measurement accuracy.
  • Installation, training, warranty and consumables supply.
  • Calibration accreditation and testing-body qualifications such as ISO/IEC 17025.

Common mistakes

  • Not checking required measurement range and accuracy.
  • Leaving consumables and calibration costs out.
  • Missing installation environment requirements such as power and temperature control.

Document checklist

  • Equipment specifications and performance test reports
  • Calibration or testing accreditation
  • Installation, training and warranty terms
  • Consumables supply plan

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

Is calibration tendered?

Yes, calibration and periodic inspection appear as service notices.

What are testing services?

Services where a provider tests or inspects products or facilities for the buyer.

What is ISO/IEC 17025?

An international standard recognizing the competence of testing and calibration laboratories.
