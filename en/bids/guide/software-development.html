Software & system development: tender guide

Tenders for building and maintaining information systems, websites, mobile apps, ERP, databases and platforms.

At a glance

Technical scoring often outweighs price, so explaining your delivery approach and comparable implementations is important.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Tenders for building information systems, websites and mobile apps, ERP, databases and platforms, maintenance, and cloud migration.
  • Typical examples are e-government systems, public service portals and business-process automation.
  • Some contracts bundle several years of maintenance and operation after delivery.

Who buys

Government ministries and agencies, municipalities, universities and international organizations.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Understanding of requirements, methodology and proposed architecture.
  • Comparable implementations and key staff experience.
  • Meeting security requirements and maintenance set-up.

Common mistakes

  • Missing mandatory items in the requirements specification.
  • Not checking data-location and security rules such as in-country storage.
  • Leaving maintenance-period costs out of the price.

Document checklist

  • Company profile and similar implementations
  • Key staff CVs
  • Technical proposal and architecture description
  • Price schedule and maintenance terms

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

Does technical quality outweigh price?

Many notices weight technical evaluation heavily. Check the evaluation criteria.

Can foreign firms bid?

It depends. Security and data rules sometimes limit bids to local firms.

Is maintenance included?

Some notices bundle delivery and maintenance. Check the scope.
