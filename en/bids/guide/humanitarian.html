Humanitarian & relief supplies: tender guide

Tenders for tents, household items, hygiene kits and water and sanitation supplies used in refugee camps, disaster zones and conflict areas. UNHCR, UNICEF, IOM and WFP are frequent buyers.

At a glance

Emergency orders often have short delivery times and demanding packing and shipping terms, so check the delivery terms (Incoterms) and destination in the notice first.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Tenders for tents and tarpaulins, blankets, kitchen sets, hygiene kits and lamps (non-food items) used in refugee and displacement camps, disaster areas and conflict zones.
  • Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) materials, shelter materials, and emergency transport and storage services also appear in this area.
  • Bulk purchases and long-term agreements (LTAs) are common, so a successful supplier can receive repeat orders.

Who buys

UNHCR, UNICEF, IOM, WFP, Red Cross–related organizations and national disaster management authorities are the main buyers.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Compliance with the technical specification (material, size, durability) and test reports is the starting point.
  • Delivery time, mass-production capacity and packing and shipping terms.
  • Price, together with past humanitarian supply references in some cases.

Common mistakes

  • Offering a specification that differs from the buyer’s technical specification and being found non-compliant.
  • Pricing freight wrongly because the Incoterms and destination were not checked.
  • Noticing a sample request too late to meet the schedule.

Document checklist

  • Product specification and test or quality reports
  • Manufacturer details and evidence of production capacity
  • References for similar supplies
  • Sample submission plan and delivery schedule

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I find humanitarian supply tenders?

UN agency tenders are gathered on UNGM and some agencies also publish on their own portals. This site collects UNGM notices daily.

Can small suppliers take part?

Yes, though large-volume capacity and quality evidence are often required, so check the minimum qualifications first.

What if the delivery time is short?

Emergency notices can have short delivery times. Work out whether production and shipping fit before bidding.
