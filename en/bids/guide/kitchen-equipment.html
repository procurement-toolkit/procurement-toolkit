Kitchen & catering equipment: tender guide

Purchase and installation tenders for commercial kitchen and canteen equipment: cooking appliances, refrigeration and freezers, dishwashers, extraction hoods and ventilation, tableware and utensils, for schools, hospitals, military and institutional kitchens.

At a glance

Notices often bundle equipment supply with installation works, and may specify electrical or gas capacity and hygiene or safety certification, so check the facility drawings and required specifications first.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Purchase and installation tenders for commercial kitchen and canteen equipment: cooking appliances, refrigeration and freezers, dishwashers, extraction hoods and ventilation, tableware and utensils.
  • When a canteen is built or refurbished, kitchen equipment is often a separate lot from the building works.
  • Some notices bundle supply, installation, commissioning, staff training and maintenance.

Who buys

School and municipal catering bodies, hospitals, military and police facilities and institutional catering contractors.

What evaluators look at

  • Compliance with specifications (capacity, electrical and gas requirements) and hygiene and safety certification.
  • Installation, commissioning and training capability, and local service set-up.
  • Total cost of ownership including energy use and upkeep in some notices.

Common mistakes

  • Proposing specifications without checking the electrical, gas and drainage conditions in the facility drawings.
  • Not checking whether hoods and installation works are a separate lot.
  • Offering equipment unsuited to local voltage or gas type.

Document checklist

  • Product specifications and hygiene and safety certificates
  • Installation and commissioning plan
  • References for similar catering equipment
  • Warranty and maintenance terms

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

Is kitchen equipment tendered separately from building works?

It may be a lot within the works or a separate supply notice.

Are hoods and ventilation included?

Some notices bundle extraction hoods with cooking equipment.

Which certifications are needed?

They differ by country and notice. Check the technical specification.
