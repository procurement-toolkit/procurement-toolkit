Grants & civil society calls: tender guide

Grant and small-funding calls aimed at civil society organizations and NGOs, issued by international organizations and public bodies. Unlike purchase tenders, the project proposal and the applicant’s eligibility are assessed.

At a glance

Check eligibility (registered organization, local track record), funding ceiling and project duration first.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Grant and small-funding calls (calls for proposals) from international organizations and public bodies for civil society organizations, NGOs and community groups.
  • Unlike purchase tenders, what is assessed is the project proposal and the organization’s capacity, not the price of goods or services.
  • Sizes range from low value grants to multi-year programmes.

Who buys

UNDP, UN funds and programmes, development agencies and national ministries publish calls as civil society support.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Fit with the call’s objectives, and clarity about beneficiaries and location.
  • Legal registration, comparable experience and financial management capacity.
  • Realistic budget, performance indicators and a monitoring plan.

Common mistakes

  • Applying without checking eligibility such as registered organization or area of operation.
  • Budget figures that do not match the activity plan.
  • Missing a template that must be completed in the local language.

Document checklist

  • Proof of registration and statutes
  • Project proposal and budget
  • Track record and financial reports
  • Representative and contact details, and bank details if requested after selection

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

Can companies apply to grant calls?

Eligibility differs by call. Many accept only civil society organizations, so check the eligibility clause first.

How is this different from a tender?

A tender competes on price and capability for defined goods or services, while a grant call funds the project you propose.

Where do I find the funding ceiling?

The call text states the ceiling and project duration, so check the original notice.
