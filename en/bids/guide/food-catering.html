Food, catering & nutrition: tender guide

Supply tenders for grain, cooking oil, processed food, nutrition supplements and catering services, from food-aid agencies, schools, hospitals and institutions.

At a glance

Shelf life, packaging, food-safety certification and origin rules often decide whether a supplier can bid.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Supply of grain, cooking oil, meat, processed food and nutrition supplements, and catering and meal services.
  • Examples include food for school, hospital, military and police catering and central-kitchen supply.
  • Bulk food purchases by food-assistance agencies are included.

Who buys

Food-assistance agencies, school and municipal catering bodies, hospitals, and military and police.

Finding notices by classification code

What evaluators look at

  • Food-safety certification and origin and traceability evidence.
  • Meeting shelf-life, packaging, storage and delivery conditions.
  • Supply reliability and price.

Common mistakes

  • Missing shelf-life and packaging requirements.
  • Not checking delivery-point schedule constraints.
  • Insufficient food-safety certification such as HACCP.

Document checklist

  • Food-safety certificates
  • Certificate of origin and quality reports
  • Delivery plan
  • Supply references

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

Are food notices framework agreements?

Catering supply is often an annual framework. Check the contract type in the notice.

What about import requirements?

Importing countries have quarantine and labelling rules. Check the notice and local rules.

Are catering services included?

Yes, meal-service notices appear too.
