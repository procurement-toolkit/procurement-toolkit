Design, supervision & engineering: tender guide

Tenders for preliminary and detailed design, construction supervision, project management consultancy and engineering advisory work. French notices often call this maîtrise d’œuvre.

At a glance

Technical scores often outweigh price, and comparable project references and the qualifications of key staff are weighed heavily.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Notices seeking firms for preliminary and detailed design, construction supervision, project management consultancy and engineering advice.
  • The French maîtrise d’œuvre is a typical label, and development-bank projects call it a design and supervision consultant.
  • These often come out before, and separately from, the works tender.

Who buys

Government ministries and municipalities, public client bodies, and World Bank and regional development bank project units.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Design and supervision references for projects of similar size and type.
  • Qualifications and experience of key staff such as the team leader.
  • Methodology, work schedule and price.

Common mistakes

  • Proposing the same key expert in several bids, which causes eligibility problems.
  • Reference evidence lacks client confirmation.
  • Mixing up design scope and supervision scope in the price.

Document checklist

  • Company registration and engineering or architectural licences
  • Evidence of similar projects
  • Key staff CVs and qualifications
  • Work plan and price schedule

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

Is competition strong?

It varies, but technical scoring is heavy, so references and team make-up matter.

Can design and supervision be bid together?

Some notices bundle them and others separate them. Check the scope in the notice.

What is maîtrise d’œuvre?

A French term for the role that leads the design and supervision of building works.
