Professional services & staffing: tender guide

Notices for engaging individual professionals or support staff on service contracts, such as Colombia’s SECOP prestación de servicios contracts and the Canadian government’s IT staffing arrangements (TBIPS).

At a glance

Contract length, fees and required qualifications are stated in the notice. Residency or nationality requirements are common, so check them first.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Notices for engaging individual professionals or support staff on service contracts, typified by Colombia’s SECOP prestación de servicios contracts and the Canadian government’s IT staffing arrangements (TBIPS).
  • Roles are broad: administrative support, legal and accounting assistance, health support such as nursing assistants, and technical support.
  • Some buyers publish large numbers of similar small contracts, which makes the count look large.

Who buys

Colombian public entities, Canadian federal departments, and European municipalities and public bodies are the usual issuers.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Whether education, experience and any licence required for the role are met.
  • Whether residency or nationality requirements apply, which should be checked first.
  • Fit between contract length, fee and scope of work and your own terms.

Common mistakes

  • Missing a residency or nationality requirement and being ineligible.
  • Not knowing the buyer’s e-procurement platform registration steps and being unable to submit.
  • Over-preparing documents for a small contract and missing the deadline.

Document checklist

  • ID and qualification evidence, and CV
  • Licences or certificates depending on the role
  • Experience evidence
  • Registration details for the relevant platform

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

Can foreigners apply?

It depends on the country and agency. Many are open only to local entities or residents, so check the eligibility clause.

What does prestación de servicios mean?

It is Spanish for provision of services, a contract form used to engage someone outside regular employment.

What is TBIPS?

The name of an arrangement the Canadian government uses to buy IT staffing services. Check the notice for detailed terms.
