Training & capacity building: tender guide

Tenders for staff training, workshops, course development and capacity-building programs.

At a glance

Audience, language and delivery mode (in person or online) vary a lot between notices, so check the scope of work first.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Tenders for staff training, workshops, course development, e-learning content and capacity-building programmes.
  • Examples include civil-servant training, teacher training, health-worker training and vocational skills training.
  • Audience, language and delivery mode (in person or online) differ a lot between notices.

Who buys

International organizations, government ministries, public bodies and development programmes.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Training design capability and trainer qualifications.
  • Comparable delivery references and how results after training are measured.
  • Local delivery capacity such as venue, interpretation and materials.

Common mistakes

  • Not reading the scope (participant numbers, sessions) precisely.
  • Leaving materials translation and printing out of the price.
  • A weak approach to assessing trainees.

Document checklist

  • Training delivery references
  • Trainer CVs
  • Course design outline
  • Delivery plan and budget

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

Is online training included?

Notices specify in-person, online or blended delivery.

Is materials development included?

Depending on scope, materials development and translation may be part of the contract.

How is impact assessed?

Some notices ask for pre- and post-training assessment in the proposal.
