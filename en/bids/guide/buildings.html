Building works & renovation: tender guide

Tenders for new construction and renovation of schools, hospitals, offices and housing. European notices (TED, BOAMP) are often split into trade lots.

At a glance

Past project references, guarantees and site-visit dates can affect eligibility, so do not miss the site-visit details in the notice.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Notices for new construction and renovation of schools, hospitals, offices and housing, building services works (plumbing, heating, electrical) and demolition.
  • European notices (TED, BOAMP) are often split into trade lots, which allows specialist firms to bid for part of the works.
  • Large works may require a prequalification step.

Who buys

Municipalities and public housing bodies, public hospitals, school owners, international organizations and development programmes.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Track record on similar works and the licence or registration for the trade.
  • Site management staff and equipment, and a realistic programme.
  • Ability to provide guarantees (bid and performance) and the price.

Common mistakes

  • Missing the site-visit or pre-bid meeting date.
  • Not checking lot-specific conditions and treating the notice as a single contract.
  • Underestimating the time to obtain bonds and missing the deadline.

Document checklist

  • Contractor registration or licence evidence
  • Completion certificates for similar works
  • Bid security and other guarantee documents
  • Programme and priced bill of quantities

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

Can foreign firms bid on EU works?

It depends on the eligibility terms of each notice and country. Read the original notice.

What is a lot?

A unit of a tender split by trade or area. You can bid for the lots you need.

Is a bid bond always required?

Whether it is required and the amount vary by notice. Check the conditions.
