Medical equipment & supplies: tender guide

Tenders for hospital equipment, surgical items, diagnostic devices and disposable medical supplies, issued steadily by public hospitals and health projects.

At a glance

Medical devices may need national registration and certifications such as CE marking or ISO 13485, so check the requirements of the delivery country.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Tenders for hospital equipment (imaging, patient monitors, ventilators), surgical supplies, disposable medical consumables and personal protective equipment (PPE).
  • Equipment is often bundled with installation, user training, warranty and maintenance.
  • Consumables may be issued as annual framework agreements.

Who buys

Public hospitals and health authorities, international organizations running health programmes, and university hospitals.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

CPV (EU) divisions
33Medical equipments, pharmaceuticals and personal care products
UNSPSC segments
42Medical Equipment and Accessories and Supplies

What evaluators look at

  • Compliance with the technical specification and medical-device certification such as CE marking or ISO 13485.
  • Installation, training and maintenance set-up, and spare-parts supply.
  • Total cost of ownership (purchase price plus running cost) in some notices.

Common mistakes

  • Missing medical-device registration requirements of the delivery country.
  • Leaving installation and training costs out of the price.
  • Not checking consumables supply terms.

Document checklist

  • Medical device licences and certificates
  • Catalogue and a specification compliance table
  • Installation, training and warranty terms
  • Evidence of local agent or service set-up

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

Is used equipment accepted?

Most notices require new equipment. Check the conditions in the notice.

Is a local service network required?

Some notices require local maintenance capability. Prepare an agent or partner plan.

Is PPE included?

Yes. Items such as masks and gloves appear here too.
