Logistics & freight services: tender guide

Tenders for road, sea and air freight, customs clearance, warehousing and courier services.

At a glance

Route, cargo weight and volume, and who handles customs clearance affect pricing, so check the expected volumes and contract period first.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Tenders for road, sea and air freight, customs clearance, warehousing, courier and relocation services.
  • Examples include long-term agreements (LTAs) for international organizations and annual transport contracts for public bodies.
  • Emergency relief transport notices have short delivery windows.

Who buys

International organizations, government bodies, public hospitals and postal or courier operators.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Freight rates and transit times by route.
  • Customs, insurance responsibilities and tracking.
  • Comparable cargo references and network.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing freight without expected volumes.
  • Not checking who bears customs duties and clearance responsibility.
  • Missing surcharge terms such as fuel adjustments.

Document checklist

  • Freight or logistics registration and insurance evidence
  • Rate schedule by route
  • Comparable shipments references
  • Description of tracking and reporting

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

How do I submit rates?

As a route-based rate table or a total price in the format the notice requires.

Who bears customs duties?

It depends on the Incoterms in the notice. Check the original.

What is an LTA?

A long-term agreement fixing rates for a period, with transport requested as needed.
