Pharmaceuticals & vaccines: tender guide

Tenders for medicines, vaccines and diagnostic reagents. Health ministries, public hospitals and health-focused international organizations are the main buyers.

At a glance

These notices often require marketing authorization, quality certification and minimum shelf-life, so read the eligibility requirements carefully.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Tenders for medicines, vaccines, diagnostic reagents and infusion fluids.
  • They include essential medicines for national health programmes, vaccines for immunization campaigns and infectious-disease test kits.
  • They are often issued as annual or multi-year supply agreements.

Who buys

National health ministries, public hospitals, central procurement agencies and health-focused international organizations such as UNICEF.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Manufacturing authorization (such as GMP), marketing authorization and quality certification.
  • Remaining shelf-life conditions and storage and transport (cold chain) capability.
  • Price, supply reliability and past supply references.

Common mistakes

  • Not checking registration requirements of the delivery country.
  • Failing the remaining shelf-life condition and being found non-compliant.
  • Leaving cold-chain freight costs out of the price.

Document checklist

  • Manufacturing licence or GMP certificate
  • Marketing authorization and quality certificates
  • Storage and transport conditions and cold-chain plan
  • Supply references

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

Can Korean manufacturers bid on foreign tenders?

Yes, but delivery-country registration and buyer prequalification may apply, so read the notice first.

Are diagnostic reagents included?

Yes. Reagents and test kits appear alongside medicines and consumables.

Where do vaccine tenders appear?

Organizations such as UNICEF and national health authorities publish them. UNGM notices are also shown on this site.
