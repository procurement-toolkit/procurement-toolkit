Solar & renewable energy: tender guide

Design, supply and installation tenders for solar PV, wind, battery storage and mini-grid projects.

At a glance

Capacity (kW, MW), performance guarantees and maintenance terms are common evaluation points, so summarize the technical requirements first.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Design, supply and installation tenders for solar PV, wind, battery energy storage and mini-grid projects.
  • Common examples are rooftop solar on public buildings, stand-alone power systems for schools and clinics, and village electrification.
  • Some bundle equipment, installation and several years of operation and maintenance.

Who buys

Energy ministries, utilities, municipalities, international organizations and development-bank project units.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Meeting the required capacity (kW, MW) and performance guarantees.
  • Certification of modules, inverters and batteries and manufacturer reliability.
  • Installation and maintenance references and local service set-up.

Common mistakes

  • Not matching warranty period and degradation terms to the notice.
  • Not checking grid-connection rules and permits.
  • Leaving out transport and site-access costs.

Document checklist

  • Equipment specifications and certificates
  • Manufacturer warranties
  • Similar installation references
  • Installation and maintenance plan

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

Are EPC notices common?

Some are EPC packages and some are equipment-only. Check the scope.

Is battery storage included?

Some notices bundle storage with solar.

What should I watch for in developing-country notices?

Local permits, customs clearance and site access should be checked early.
