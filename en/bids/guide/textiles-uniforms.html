Uniforms, clothing & textiles: tender guide

Supply tenders for uniforms, workwear, footwear, blankets and mattresses.

At a glance

Samples, fabric and colour specifications and size distribution are common requirements, so plan your sampling timeline early.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Supply tenders for uniforms, workwear, footwear, blankets, mattresses and bedding.
  • Police, fire, hospital and school uniforms and relief blankets are common.
  • Many are institutional clothing with fixed designs and size distributions.

Who buys

Police, military and fire services, hospitals, schools and international organizations.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Compliance with fabric, colour and sewing specifications, and sample approval.
  • Handling the size distribution and mass-production capacity.
  • Delivery time and quality inspection.

Common mistakes

  • Not allowing time for sample production.
  • Not checking size distribution and repeat-order terms.
  • Having no fabric test reports (durability, colour fastness).

Document checklist

  • Fabric specifications and test reports
  • Samples and production schedule
  • Evidence of production capacity
  • Supply references

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Many notices require sample approval before production.

How is the size distribution handled?

The notice states quantities by size, which go into the production plan.
