Printing, media, communications & translation: tender guide

Tenders for printing, publishing, communications and advertising, video and photography, design, and translation and interpretation services.

At a glance

Translation and communications work usually asks for language pairs, volumes, deadlines and samples of earlier work.

What notices appear in this sector

  • Tenders for printing, publishing, communications and advertising, web marketing, video and photography, design, and translation and interpretation.
  • Campaign planning, promotional video production, media support and communications consulting are included.
  • Language pairs, volumes and deadlines are often the core of the evaluation.

Who buys

Government bodies, municipalities, communications departments of international organizations and public companies.

Finding notices by classification code

When a title does not make the sector obvious, classification codes help. European notices use the Common Procurement Vocabulary (CPV), while international organizations mostly use UNSPSC. The closest categories for this sector are:

What evaluators look at

  • Past work (portfolio) and quality.
  • Language capability and review and quality control.
  • Delivery time and unit rates (per word or page).

Common mistakes

  • Not checking language pairs and review requirements.
  • Missing print specifications (paper, finishing) and quantities.
  • Not reviewing copyright and usage-right terms.

Document checklist

  • Portfolio and earlier work
  • Translator or designer CVs
  • Description of quality control procedure
  • Rate card

How a tender usually runs

  1. Find the notice — Check new notices daily and read the deadline, eligibility and submission method first.
  2. Eligibility and registration — Many buyers require registration on their e-procurement system or a platform such as UNGM. It takes time, so register early.
  3. Clarifications — Ask about unclear clauses in writing during the clarification period. Answers are shared with all bidders.
  4. Submission — Submit the technical and price proposals in the required format before the deadline, which may be in local time.
  5. Evaluation — Evaluation often goes eligibility, then technical, then price, and some notices do not award on price alone.
  6. Award and contract — After award notification the contract terms are agreed and signed. A guarantee may be required.

Frequently asked questions

Are translation rates per word?

Some notices use per word, per page or lump sum. Check the price format.

Who owns the copyright?

It depends on the notice and contract. Check the usage-rights clause.

Is interpretation included?

Yes, conference interpretation and translation appear together.
